This case study examines a weighted-apparel Meta Ads account across campaigns, audiences and creatives—showing where purchase volume came from, which audience groups reduced acquisition cost, and how newer photo and lifestyle concepts created a stronger testing path.

The challenge

Sports audiences could produce volume, but cost efficiency varied sharply.

  • Baseball, softball and parent-oriented campaigns generated purchases but often at higher acquisition cost.
  • Broader and non-traditional segments—lookalikes, busy moms and healthcare workers—showed stronger purchase efficiency in the supplied data.
  • Creative performance varied significantly even inside the same audience, making ad-level testing essential.
  • The account contains mixed objectives such as profile visits, link clicks and purchases, so purchase analysis must be kept separate from traffic metrics.
Optimization direction

Use audience expansion and creative testing without abandoning proven volume.

  • Keep high-volume sports segments as benchmarks rather than the only prospecting strategy.
  • Scale lower-CPP audience pockets gradually while protecting measurement quality.
  • Test everyday-movement, healthcare-worker and lifestyle hooks alongside sports-led creative.
  • Validate unusually high Meta-reported ROAS against backend/store revenue before using it as a scaling signal.

What the data suggests

A practical testing-to-scaling framework for Kilogear.

1. Keep sports audiences as volume benchmarks.

Dads Baseball/Softball produced 52 purchases, so the segment has proven demand. The goal is not to remove it blindly; it is to compare every new audience against its cost and conversion quality.

2. Expand non-athlete use cases.

Healthcare-worker and busy-mom data support testing “wear your workout,” daily movement and workday activity angles—not only competitive-sports messaging.

3. Separate traffic from purchase reporting.

The account includes a giveaway/profile-visit campaign and a catalogue link-click campaign. These should not be blended into purchase CPA reporting.

4. Validate purchase value before scaling from ROAS.

Some Meta-reported ROAS values are extreme. Use Meta for directional diagnosis, then reconcile with store revenue, returns and margin before making budget decisions.
