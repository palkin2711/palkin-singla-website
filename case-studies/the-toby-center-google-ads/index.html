The Toby Center · Google Ads · Supervised visitation services

Scaling awareness and qualified search visibility for supervised visitation services.

This case study documents a Google Ads account built around supervised visitation, safe visitation-center services and family-transition intent. The supplied reports show a mix of Performance Max and Search activity designed to expand visibility while also capturing high-intent searches around court-ordered visits, visitation monitoring and child-exchange services.

High-level Google Ads summary

574 clicks from 4.26K impressions at an average CPC of $9.40.

The supplied Google Ads overview screenshot shows 574 clicks, 4.26K impressions, $9.40 average CPC and approximately $5.4K in spend across the full reporting period. At the campaign level, almost all spend came from the Performance Max campaign, while the Search campaign supplied a smaller but still relevant layer of supervised-visitation intent coverage.

Search insight

Brand + service-intent search behavior worked together.

  • Brand demand is visible through searches such as “the toby center” and “toby center.”
  • High-intent service demand appears in searches like “supervised visitation,” “affordable supervised visitation” and “places that do supervised visitation near me.”
  • Family-transition positioning also shows up through “toby center for family transitions.”
  • This mix suggests the account was supporting both brand discovery and active service research.

Audience and behavior insights

Desktop dominated, weekday daytime activity was strongest, and known impressions skewed toward women 35–44.

Devices

  • Computers drove 82.4% of cost, 84.4% of impressions and 84.5% of clicks.
  • Mobile phones contributed 17.5% of cost and 15.3% of clicks.
  • Tablets were negligible at about 0.2–0.3% across the main device metrics.
  • This account clearly performed as a desktop-first local service search journey.

Day & hour + demographics

  • The heatmap shows the strongest activity during weekday late morning through late afternoon, especially around the midday block.
  • Known demographic impressions were most concentrated in the 35–44 age bracket, with the darkest visible segment on the female side.
  • Secondary visibility appears across 45–54 and male 35–54 cohorts.
  • Because the widget notes only 66% of impressions had known age/gender data, this should be used directionally.
Ad relevance

The most-shown search ad aligned strongly with the core service promise.

The leading ad focused on supervised visitation, court-ordered visits and safe visitation-center services, supported by trust-building messaging around trained monitors, neutral settings and structured scheduling.

Why that matters

  • The headline speaks directly to a very specific legal/family-service use case.
  • The description reinforces safety, structure and the child’s well-being—strong intent-matching language for this niche.
  • The ad achieved 35 clicks from 302 impressions for an 11.59% CTR.

Practical takeaway

  • Service-specific copy can outperform generic family-service messaging in sensitive categories like supervised visitation.
  • Combining this messaging with refined keyword expansion around exchanges, monitors and visitation-center intent could deepen coverage further.
