Building a measurable path from traffic to calls and appointments.

This account evolved beyond simple website traffic. The campaign history includes landing-page acquisition, retargeting, appointment campaigns and call-focused healthcare offers—allowing performance to be evaluated at multiple stages of the patient inquiry journey.

Full-funnel account structure

Traffic, retargeting, appointments and calls were measured as separate outcomes.

The campaign export documents 5,647 link-click results, 2,618 landing-page-view results, 405 native call placements, 66 website-schedule results and 40 service-specific call/schedule actions.

The challenge

Different healthcare services needed different conversion paths.

  • Traffic campaigns were useful for building low-cost site visits and retargeting pools, but traffic alone was not the final KPI.
  • Appointment-focused services needed schedule events, while urgent/high-intent services were better evaluated on calls.
  • Campaigns for telemedicine, abortion-pill inquiries, STD/UTI services and general booking calls required separate cost benchmarks.
  • Creative and language tests needed to be evaluated against the correct conversion event—not against one blended result column.
Optimization approach

Move budget closer to the actions that represent real patient intent.

  • Keep traffic acquisition separate from call and appointment reporting.
  • Use retargeting for visitors who need additional trust before booking.
  • Test service-specific creative, language and audience combinations.
  • Compare call cost, schedule cost and landing-page efficiency within the same objective type.

Call efficiency

Telemedicine call acquisition was about 33.1% lower in cost.

The Telemedicine / Abortion Pill campaign generated 217 calls at $14.24 per call versus 188 calls at $21.28 for the broader Booking Calls campaign.

  • Cost per call was approximately 33.1% lower.
  • The telemedicine campaign also generated 29 more calls while spending about $910 less.
  • This is a documented cross-campaign efficiency comparison, not a controlled causal test.

Spanish test vs main telemedicine ad set

The Spanish test recorded $11.77 per call versus $14.82 for the main Telemedicine ad set—about 20.6% lower. The test had lower volume, so it is best treated as a scaling signal rather than proof that language alone caused the difference.

Telemedicine vs general LA call set

The main Telemedicine ad set’s $14.82 call cost was about 29.2% lower than the $20.94 LA call ad set, supporting continued service-specific campaign segmentation.

Warm-audience appointment efficiency

MOF / website-visitor leads reduced schedule cost.

The MOF lead campaign generated 34 website schedules at $34.21 each, compared with 32 schedules at $44.38 for the broader appointment-booking campaign.
