Tinnitus Help App · Google Ads · App acquisition

Scaling mobile-app discovery while capturing high-intent tinnitus searches.

This Google Ads account combined Android app-install campaigns with a smaller UK Search campaign. The result was a broad acquisition layer across Google’s app inventory, supported by search ads that matched people actively researching tinnitus relief, ear-ringing support and sound-therapy solutions.

Account-level performance

471 documented installs from £924 in spend.

The supplied overview shows 3.58K clicks, 143K impressions, 471 installs and £924 in cost. Using the campaign export totals, the account averaged about £0.26 per click. A simple spend-to-install calculation gives an approximate £1.96 cost per documented install across the account period.

Scale vs intent

The account used app campaigns for volume and Search for concentrated intent.

  • Android App Install 4/5 produced 2,893 clicks and represented about 81.7% of total spend.
  • Tinnitus App - UK Search had much lower volume but the strongest campaign CTR at 15.76%.
  • tinnitushelp for android delivered a 5.24% CTR at an approximate £0.18 CPC, the lowest traffic cost among the five campaign rows.
  • The structure separated broad app discovery from direct symptom/help search intent instead of forcing both behaviors into one campaign.

Search-intent layer

The Search campaign matched people actively looking for ways to manage ear ringing.

The search-term export contains 78 queries. The most visible themes were treatment, relief, stopping ear ringing and questions about whether tinnitus can be improved. These terms reflect user intent; they are not medical-efficacy claims.

Keyword performance

  • “how to stop ear ringing” generated 29 clicks from £18.13 in spend.
  • The keyword widget reports a 16.76% CTR.
  • Other enabled themes included tinnitus sound therapy, tinnitus masking sounds, white noise for tinnitus and tinnitus relief app.

Search-term quality

  • The 78-query export logged 154 impressions and 25 clicks.
  • Search-term spend totalled £16.19.
  • The supplied search-term report shows 0 tracked conversions, so this page does not claim Search-driven installs.
Device & network behavior

Acquisition was overwhelmingly mobile and largely delivered through Display inventory.

Devices

  • Mobile phones: 90.6% of cost, 88.6% of impressions and 85.8% of clicks.
  • Tablets: 9.3% of cost, 11.4% of impressions and 14.2% of clicks.
  • Computers: only 0.1% of cost and essentially no click share in the widget.
  • This device mix is consistent with a campaign designed primarily around mobile-app discovery and installation.

Networks

  • Display network: 88.3% of clicks and 87.7% of cost, with £0.26 average CPC.
  • Google Search: 8.8% of clicks and 10.4% of cost, with £0.30 average CPC.
  • YouTube: 2.8% of clicks and 1.9% of cost, with £0.18 average CPC.
  • Search partners: about 0.1% of clicks and negligible cost share.
Audience timing & demographics

Visibility was broad through the week, with older age groups prominent in known demographic data.

Day & hour

  • Impressions were distributed across most days and daytime/evening hours.
  • The heatmap shows a visible peak around Monday late afternoon.
  • Another stronger pocket appears around Friday evening.
  • This suggests the app was discoverable across multiple daily-use windows rather than a narrow appointment-time pattern.

Demographics

  • The widget is based on the 59% of impressions with known age and gender.
  • The strongest visible concentration is in the 65+ group, especially among women.
  • Female 55–64 and male 65+ also show meaningful impression density.
  • These patterns are directional only because 41% of impressions were not represented in the known demographic breakdown.
Search ad relevance

The UK Search ad matched the user’s immediate problem language.

The most-shown Search ad promoted a tinnitus-support app using messaging around ear ringing, personalised sound therapy, features, success stories and how the app works. This describes the ad’s positioning; it is not an independent medical claim about treatment efficacy.
