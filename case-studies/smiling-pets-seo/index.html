eCommerce SEO · Pet Supplies · India

eCommerce SEO growth for an online pet store.

Smiling Pets is an eCommerce pet-supplies business selling pet food, treats, toys, grooming products and everyday essentials across India. The eCommerce SEO strategy focused on improving organic visibility for collection pages, category pages, product-led searches and high-intent “buy online / cash on delivery” queries while keeping the shopping journey useful for real pet parents.

Why the ranking shift matters

The account moved from an average position just outside the first page toward an average position close to the first-page boundary. More importantly, several commercial pet-food and cash-on-delivery queries moved into clear page-one territory.

The eCommerce SEO challenge

eCommerce SEO had to balance category depth, product intent and page overlap.

Smiling Pets carries dog, cat, bird, fish and small-pet products, with a large mix of brand, category and individual product pages. SEO therefore needed to support broad pet-store intent without allowing similar collections and products to compete for the same search terms.

eCommerce SEO scope

This project covered collection-page SEO, commercial keyword targeting, category architecture, internal linking, search snippet improvement and organic visibility for high-intent shopping searches. The objective was not only to attract more traffic, but to bring searchers directly into useful product and collection journeys.

Commercial-intent SEO

  • Target searches such as pet shop online cash on delivery and buy pets online India cash on delivery.
  • Strengthen collection pages for pet-food brands and product types.
  • Connect high-intent landing pages with relevant collections and products.
  • Improve search-result relevance through stronger titles, headings and page context.

Category & product SEO

  • Build visibility around cat food, dog food, grooming and brand-led queries.
  • Use category/collection pages as durable search landing pages rather than relying only on individual products.
  • Improve internal discovery between collections, products and informational content.
  • Support local/store relevance alongside pan-India eCommerce visibility.

Search growth concentrated around intent-specific pages instead of generic traffic alone.

The strongest pattern in the export is not just “more traffic.” It is improved visibility for pages and queries that are closer to purchase intent: pet-food brands, collections, grooming products and online/COD shopping searches.

Next eCommerce SEO opportunities

There is still meaningful upside in CTR, collection depth and page-two terms.

The current data shows stronger rankings, but some high-impression collection and product queries still sit near positions 8–15 with low CTR. Those are useful next-step opportunities because modest ranking and snippet improvements can produce additional qualified traffic without needing entirely new topics.

Priority growth work

  • Push high-impression terms in positions 8–15 further into the top results.
  • Improve titles and snippets for collection pages with strong impressions but low CTR.
  • Expand internal links from blogs and category hubs toward commercial collections.
  • Continue strengthening local pages for Mumbai, Thane and Navi Mumbai where relevant.

Measurement priorities

  • Separate branded vs non-branded search reporting.
  • Track revenue and transactions by organic landing page.
  • Monitor search appearance changes for product snippets and merchant listings.
  • Audit GA4 direct-traffic spikes before using site-wide user growth in performance reporting.
eCommerce SEO case study summary

eCommerce SEO improved visibility around pages with real shopping intent.

The strongest documented outcomes are improved average position, better CTR, first-page rankings for multiple pet-product terms, and substantial page-level gains across COD landing pages and priority collections.
