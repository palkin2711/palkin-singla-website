AI-driven discovery is changing the questions customers ask, but it has not made good SEO obsolete. Business owners want to know how to rank in ChatGPT, appear as a supporting source in Google AI Overviews and earn mentions in Perplexity. These experiences use different retrieval and answer-generation systems, so there is no single submission form or guaranteed ranking position. A sustainable approach is to create genuinely useful, discoverable information that clearly explains who you help, which problems you solve and why a reader should trust the answer.

What “ranking” in AI search actually means

A traditional search result has visible positions; an AI answer may cite, link to or summarize multiple sources. A brand can be mentioned without a clickable citation, and a cited page can differ across prompts. Separate three outcomes in reporting: source citations, branded mentions and qualified visits. A page that appears once for a broad prompt is not proof of lasting visibility. Check repeatability using a stable set of customer questions and review whether those appearances lead to meaningful enquiries.

Action you can take

Create a worksheet with a question, platform, date, cited URL, mention type and next action. Test queries across research, comparison and purchase intent.

Start with technical eligibility and crawlability

Google states that AI Overviews and AI Mode use normal Search indexing eligibility rather than special AI-only markup. Important pages should return a successful HTTP status, allow relevant crawlers and provide readable content in HTML. Confirm that canonical tags point to preferred URLs, XML sitemaps are current and internal links help visitors find supporting pages. A visually attractive page that hides its core facts in inaccessible interactions gives both people and retrieval systems less to work with.

Action you can take

Inspect Search Console indexing, robots directives, sitemap coverage and mobile rendering before rewriting hundreds of headings.

Answer real buyer questions with specific evidence

AI search tends to be used for multi-part questions: what works, what it costs, which option fits a constraint and what trade-offs exist. A thin page repeating a keyword is less useful than a clear explanation with selection criteria, limitations and a worked example. For a local service provider, answer how to compare proposals, typical onboarding steps and which questions to ask an agency. Do not invent benchmark results or claim direct client outcomes without evidence.

Action you can take

Turn sales calls and support questions into a question bank, then prioritize those with a clear business decision behind them.

Use clear entities, definitions and context

An entity is a recognizable person, service, organization or concept—not just a repeated phrase. Make your business identity consistent across the website, professional profiles and relevant listings. Define abbreviations such as GEO, CRO and GA4 when first used. Connect related concepts naturally: a page about AI search should explain its relationship to structured data, crawling, content quality and conventional rankings. This helps readers understand the subject without turning every paragraph into a keyword list.

Action you can take

Audit the About page, service pages and author bio for consistent descriptions of expertise and offered services.

Build topical depth without publishing near-duplicates

One massive general guide rarely answers every follow-up well. Create a helpful central resource and link to distinct, deeper articles when the reader needs a step-by-step process. An AI search guide can link to service-business SEO, technical indexing, content audits and measurement. Avoid creating ten pages that differ only by substituting ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity in the title. Repetition wastes editorial effort and confuses the audience about which article is definitive.

Action you can take

Map each article to one unique reader question and a clear next step, then consolidate overlapping pages.

Show who wrote the advice and how it was evaluated

E-E-A-T is a way to think about experience, expertise, authority and trust; it is not a switch that guarantees placement. Put a real byline and relevant background on the article. Explain the scope of examples, name the platform documentation behind technical statements and distinguish a recommendation from a tested result. For high-stakes topics, corroboration and careful qualifications matter especially. A credible author page is useful because visitors can assess the source independently.

Action you can take

Add an author bio, publication date and a “last reviewed” process to pages that discuss frequently changing products.

Structure answers for scanning, not for a secret algorithm

Start important sections with a direct answer, then expand with reasoning, examples and practical implementation. Use descriptive H2 headings, tables when comparing choices, short lists for procedures and explicit explanations of exceptions. This format improves human usability and can make passages easier for retrieval systems to interpret. It does not guarantee a citation. Artificially chopping every answer into identical fragments or generating excessive FAQs creates more content without necessarily adding value.

Action you can take

Rewrite one article section as: direct answer, who it applies to, process, limitation and example.

Apply structured data accurately

Article, BreadcrumbList and Person markup can clarify information already visible on a page. Match names, dates, images and URLs to the actual article. Structured data is not an AI citation shortcut, and adding unsupported FAQ markup does not create a promise of enhanced search display. Use a validator to check syntax, but also verify that the marked-up claims are factually present.

Action you can take

Audit JSON-LD publication dates, canonical URLs and author names whenever templates change.

Measure visibility and qualified business impact

Use Search Console for relevant Google search performance, web analytics for landing pages and enquiries, and a small recurring prompt-monitoring log for AI answer observations. Referral attribution may be incomplete because platforms and browsers vary. Report citations and sessions separately from lead quality and revenue. Compare changes after substantial content improvements rather than declaring success after a single AI mention.

Action you can take

Review a fixed set of fifteen high-intent customer prompts monthly and record actual supporting links.

A 30-day practical implementation plan

Week one: audit crawlability, canonicalization and priority pages. Week two: publish answers to overlooked buyer questions. Week three: improve author information, sources and contextual linking. Week four: validate indexing, track referrals and identify sections needing better examples. If your team has limited capacity, prioritize the pages already receiving qualified impressions or enquiries rather than launching an entirely new microsite.

Action you can take

Create one owner and one measurable outcome for each task; keep the plan grounded in resources you can maintain.

Distinguish platform-specific discovery

ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI experiences do not operate as one unified search database. Features can vary by product, region, query and mode. Some experiences consult web results; others answer from model knowledge or different retrieval tools. Do not extrapolate Google indexing requirements to every assistant. Instead verify what is publicly documented for each platform and monitor your own citations in a consistent manner.

Action you can take

Review each platform as a separate discovery channel and record the exact query plus observed sources.

Service-business example: choosing an SEO provider

Imagine a founder asks an AI assistant how to evaluate SEO proposals for a regional business. A useful article would compare technical audits, content work, local visibility, reporting methods and warning signs. It should state that no legitimate provider can guarantee rankings. The source earns reader trust by helping compare decisions, not by repeating “best SEO agency” in every heading.

Frequently asked questions

Can I guarantee my site will be cited by ChatGPT?

No. AI responses vary by query, retrieval availability and product behavior. Focus on access, usefulness and measurable visibility rather than promises.

Does Google require special GEO schema?

No. Google says its normal Search eligibility and helpful-content guidance apply to AI features.

Should I add an llms.txt file?

Do not treat it as a ranking requirement. Prioritize accessible content, crawlability and authoritative answers.

Is traditional SEO still important?

Yes. Technical access, relevant content and internal linking remain foundational.

How often should I update AI SEO articles?

Review when platform documentation changes or examples become inaccurate, not simply to change the date.

How can I report Perplexity mentions?

Track citations and referral sessions alongside a repeatable prompt set; avoid interpreting one test as a market-wide result.
