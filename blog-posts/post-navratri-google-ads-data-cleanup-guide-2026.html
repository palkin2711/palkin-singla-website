Why the clean-up week matters

Seasonal campaigns create useful data quickly, but they also create noise: unusual search terms, one-time buyers, coupon traffic, duplicate leads, spikes in direct traffic and short-lived creative performance. The week after Navratri is the right time to separate durable learning from temporary behavior. Do not judge the entire campaign by one blended ROAS or CPL. Break the result into search intent, audience, creative, landing page and lead quality.

Review search terms systematically

Sort search terms by spend, conversions, conversion value and relevance. High-spend non-converting terms deserve attention, but do not add negatives purely because a query has zero conversions in a small sample. Look for clear intent mismatches, employment queries, information-only searches, unrelated geographies, DIY intent or products you do not sell. Group patterns before applying broad negative themes.

Build negative keyword lists with care

Shared negative lists can protect multiple campaigns from recurring waste, but aggressive negatives can block valuable long-tail searches. Choose match type deliberately and review the actual queries affected. For seasonal terms, decide whether they should be paused temporarily or excluded permanently. The goal is not to make the account look cleaner; it is to preserve useful demand while removing obvious waste.

Identify junk-lead patterns

For lead campaigns, compare form data with CRM outcomes. Look for fake details, out-of-area submissions, job seekers, vendors, repeated contacts, low-value service requests or leads that never answer. Then identify which campaigns, keywords, placements or forms generated them. A junk lead is a quality problem that should be traced back to its source, not simply deleted from a spreadsheet.

Compare festival traffic with a sensible baseline

Compare against the previous period, same weekdays and, where available, the previous seasonal event. A holiday week often changes weekday patterns, so a simple seven-day comparison can mislead. Look at conversion rate, revenue per session, qualified-lead rate, average order value and new-versus-returning behavior. This shows whether higher traffic was actually better traffic.

Clean audiences and remarketing logic

Review website visitor audiences, customer lists and engagement audiences. Exclude converters where appropriate, respect consent requirements, and remove obsolete seasonal segments if they will not be reused. Keep valuable high-intent audiences for Diwali remarketing, but change the message. A user who already saw a Navratri offer should not keep receiving an expired creative.

Audit creative and landing pages

Mark which ads are still safe to run and which contain expired dates, prices, coupon codes or festival-specific promises. Remove or pause outdated landing-page banners. Redirect temporary campaign URLs only when appropriate; if a page has useful evergreen content, update it instead of deleting it blindly. Broken post-festival journeys create wasted spend and poor user experience.

Reconcile conversions across systems

Compare ad-platform conversions with GA4, ecommerce orders and CRM records. Differences are normal because attribution systems use different logic, but large unexplained gaps need investigation. Check duplicate tags, missing transaction IDs, offline imports and consent effects. The objective is not to force every platform to show the same number; it is to understand why they differ.

Create a short learning memo

Summarize what scaled, what failed, which audience was strongest, which queries appeared, what the lead-quality pattern looked like and what should change before the next festival. Include numbers and decisions, not just observations. Execution note: keep the campaign operationally simple

A short festive window rewards teams that can read performance quickly and make controlled decisions. Keep naming conventions clear, preserve a change log, confirm who owns creative updates and website changes, and agree on the metric that determines whether the campaign should scale, hold or pause. This discipline prevents a common problem in seasonal marketing: too many people making simultaneous edits based on different dashboards. A simple operating process also makes the post-campaign review more reliable because the team can connect each performance shift to a known action, offer change or audience condition.

Final takeaway

Festive marketing performs best when urgency is supported by operational readiness. Keep the offer accurate, protect measurement, document campaign changes and make the next step easy for the customer. The goal is not a short burst of activity; it is a controlled campaign that produces useful revenue or lead data and leaves the account stronger for the next seasonal peak.
