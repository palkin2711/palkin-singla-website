Black Friday falls on Friday, November 27, 2026, followed by Cyber Monday on November 30. Customers will see competing promotions everywhere, but a louder discount is not automatically a better strategy. The businesses most likely to protect their margins are the ones that define the offer, landing page, measurement and operational capacity before scaling advertising. This guide lays out a practical cross-channel plan for businesses with modest or growing budgets.

Set the commercial goal before the promotional headline

Begin with gross margin, stock availability, fulfilment capacity, average order value and the number of profitable orders you can support. A 30% discount on a low-margin product can attract orders while destroying contribution profit. Distinguish customer acquisition from repeat-customer revenue, because the acceptable cost per order may differ. Define a target that includes shipping, returns and promotion costs rather than using revenue alone to call the campaign a success.

Action you can take

Calculate contribution margin per promotional order and use it to set a realistic acquisition-cost ceiling.

Design one understandable offer architecture

Buyers should be able to explain the offer after one glance. Consider a sitewide percentage discount, tiered thresholds, bundles or an exclusive product offer. Avoid stacking multiple codes whose conditions are hard to understand. Put exclusions, end dates and delivery expectations where customers can find them. A bundle may add perceived value without reducing the headline price as sharply as a broad markdown.

Action you can take

Write the offer as one short sentence, then check it against your margin model and inventory.

Build a campaign calendar around November 27 and 30

The warm-up phase should build awareness and test message clarity. The launch phase should direct spend toward the proven offer and high-intent visitors. Cyber Monday can feature an alternative angle if inventory and margin allow. Do not leave measurement validation or creative approvals until the evening before launch. Treat the calendar as an operating plan: assign one owner for site updates, customer communications and ad monitoring.

Action you can take

Schedule prelaunch QA, launch checks, mid-event reviews, fulfilment checks and a post-event reporting meeting.

Google Ads: protect high-intent discovery

Search can capture shoppers who already know the product category or brand. Audit search terms, final URLs, ad assets, promotion messaging and product availability. For Shopping or Performance Max, verify feed prices and stock statuses match the website. Do not route deal-specific keywords to an unrelated homepage. Budget changes should consider query quality and observed conversion economics rather than an assumption that every holiday click is valuable.

Action you can take

Prepare an offer-specific landing page and monitor brand versus non-brand traffic separately.

Meta Ads: pair distinct creative angles with buyer stages

Build creative around different buying motivations: a gift solution, practical benefit, product demonstration, customer reassurance and a time-limited offer. Make terms legible on mobile. Use prospecting to introduce the promotion and retargeting to resolve hesitation among eligible visitors. Avoid claiming a countdown is ending when the offer will continue unchanged; false scarcity can damage trust.

Action you can take

Create at least three genuinely different messages rather than ten crops of the same graphic.

Use email without overwhelming the list

Segment current customers, subscribers who have not purchased and recent customers with relevant product interests. Explain the offer clearly, provide a direct link and suppress recent purchasers from redundant reminders where appropriate. A welcome or abandoned-checkout flow should not conflict with the event-wide discount. Respect consent and unsubscribe requirements for each market.

Action you can take

Prepare launch, reminder and last-day messages with clear eligibility and operationally accurate delivery promises.

Make the landing page easy to complete

Put the offer terms, relevant products, trust information and a visible CTA near the top. Confirm that promo codes apply correctly across currencies, devices and cart combinations. Test page speed, checkout failures and out-of-stock behavior. A sudden traffic spike exposes problems that everyday browsing may not reveal. Have a fallback plan for broken banners, pricing mistakes or unavailable products.

Action you can take

Run a complete mobile order test including a discount, shipping address, payment and confirmation.

Allocate budget with a decision framework

A sample allocation can reserve the largest share for historically profitable acquisition, a smaller amount for warm audiences and a controlled creative-testing reserve. These are planning buckets, not universal percentages. If the campaign has little trustworthy purchase data, increase caution rather than guessing a 4x ROAS. Watch daily contribution margin, not only platform-reported revenue.

Action you can take

Set daily spending ceilings and a clearly defined condition for increasing or holding spend.

Measure each channel on consistent business outcomes

Define which conversions count and check Google Ads, Meta Ads, GA4 and the store platform for event consistency. The same order may appear in multiple advertising dashboards because attribution is not additive. Separate channel-reported results from unique orders and contribution profit. Watch refund trends, repeat orders and stock issues alongside immediate sales.

Action you can take

Create a daily sheet with spend, sessions, orders, net revenue, estimated margin and tracking notes.

Plan for the week after the event

A profitable promotion creates follow-up work: send fulfilment updates, handle returns, request appropriate feedback and develop retention campaigns. Analyze which offer and product combination attracted customers you actually want to keep. Reuse learning in future launches, but avoid presenting holiday costs as normal evergreen benchmarks.

Action you can take

Document winning creative angles, landing-page changes and any customer-service problems within one week.

Prepare risk controls for a busy trading period

Large promotions create operational risk. Assign owners for pricing validation, fulfilment updates, customer support, payment monitoring and ad approvals. Keep a rollback plan for incorrect discounts and stock mismatches. Some customers arrive from social screenshots after an offer has changed, so communication must be consistent. A sale that damages trust through confusion can undermine future retention.

Action you can take

Write a one-page incident checklist with escalation contacts and the exact customer-facing correction process.

Example: protecting a bundle margin

Suppose a store has an accessory with a low individual margin and a related core product with more room for promotion. A carefully priced bundle might deliver a better order contribution than a sitewide markdown. Before advertising, calculate product cost, discount, payment fees, shipping subsidy and expected return costs. Then check that the same offer displays consistently across ads, email and checkout.

Frequently asked questions

When is Black Friday 2026?

Friday, November 27, 2026. Cyber Monday follows on Monday, November 30.

How early should a small business prepare?

Begin as early as practical with offer economics and tracking, then reserve time for creative and checkout QA.

Do I need a bigger ad budget for Black Friday?

Not necessarily. Increase only where your margins, inventory and conversion evidence justify it.

Should I use Google Ads or Meta Ads?

Google can capture existing intent; Meta can introduce offers and reconnect with engaged audiences.

What is the most important Black Friday KPI?

Contribution profit and qualified new customers matter more than clicks or gross revenue alone.

Should I run different offers on Cyber Monday?

Only when the change is operationally clear and economically justified; avoid confusing overlapping promotions.
