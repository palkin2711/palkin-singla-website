Diwali falls on November 8, 2026, which means the final days of October are a critical preparation window. Waiting until the festival week to build campaigns can create avoidable problems with creative approvals, inventory, delivery dates, tracking and remarketing.

1. Lock the offer before scaling media

Decide the commercial offer first: discount, bundle, gift, free shipping, appointment package or limited collection. Paid media cannot rescue an unclear promotion.

2. Build a creative system, not one festive ad

Prepare multiple angles:

  • Gift guide
  • Best sellers
  • New festive collection
  • Customer proof
  • Urgency and shipping deadline
  • Last-minute option

3. Separate prospecting and remarketing

Prospecting should introduce the product and festive reason to buy. Remarketing can use stronger urgency based on what the user already viewed.

4. Review Google Ads search demand

Check whether your category has genuine Diwali-related search intent. Add seasonal terms only when the landing page and product match the query.

5. Update Merchant Center and product feeds

For ecommerce advertisers, check titles, availability, pricing, images and promotional information before peak traffic. Feed errors during the festival window can reduce campaign coverage.

6. Prepare landing pages and collections

The ad promise must continue on the page. Make shipping deadlines, stock, returns, store information and offer terms easy to find.

7. Verify tracking before spend increases

  • Purchase or lead conversion fires once
  • Revenue/value is correct
  • UTMs are consistent
  • Calls are tracked where relevant
  • CRM source capture works
  • Remarketing audiences are populating

8. Plan budget by phase

Do not spend the full budget too early. Reserve budget for the highest-intent period, but account for shipping or service-capacity limits. A late surge is useless if orders cannot be fulfilled.

9. Prepare post-Diwali retention

Seasonal acquisition is more valuable when new customers return. Prepare email flows, review requests, cross-sell offers and audience exclusions before the campaign ends.

10. Use October campaign learnings

Navratri, Dussehra and Karwa Chauth campaigns can reveal which festive creative angles, products and audience signals are strongest. Use those insights rather than starting Diwali campaigns from zero.

Segment by customer intent, not only by platform

Think in terms of cold discovery, product consideration, high-intent search, cart or form abandonment, past customers and last-minute buyers. Google, Meta, email and organic content can each support different stages of that journey.

Prepare customer-service capacity

Higher ad spend can create more questions, calls, delivery changes and support requests. Make sure response times and stock information can handle the expected volume. A campaign that generates demand faster than the business can serve it can damage customer experience.

Build a post-festival reporting template

Compare spend, revenue, margin, new-customer share, repeat-customer share, qualified lead rate, creative angle, product category and channel. Document the top three lessons while the campaign is still fresh. Those learnings should become the starting point for the next seasonal period, not disappear inside a platform report.

FAQ

When should Diwali ads start in 2026?

Preparation should be complete by late October for many brands. Exact launch timing depends on product lead time, delivery, customer consideration and inventory.

Should I increase budget every day near Diwali?

Only when performance and operational capacity support it. Scaling should consider profitability, stock and fulfilment—not just demand.

What should local service businesses promote?

Use relevant pre-festival needs such as beauty appointments, cleaning, décor, gifting, photography or events. Avoid unrelated festival offers.
